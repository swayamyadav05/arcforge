# LinkedIn Voice Guide — Founder POV

## Audience
Fellow founders, indie hackers, engineers, early-stage investors, people who find solo-founder journeys interesting. NOT HR professionals or B2B SaaS marketers. Write for the former, ignore the latter.

## Voice rules

- **First-person, always**. "I decided..." not "We decided..." You are a solo founder; act like it.
- **Specific over abstract**. "I chose Neon over Supabase because Prisma tooling is where my comfort lives" > "I chose a database I was familiar with."
- **Vulnerable, not performatively vulnerable**. "I almost killed this decision three times" is honest. "My biggest failure taught me my biggest lesson" is cringe.
- **150-300 words**. LinkedIn algorithm rewards longer posts than Twitter, but don't pad.
- **No hooks like "3 lessons I learned"**, "Here's what nobody tells you", "Unpopular opinion:", "🚀 Excited to share".
- **Opening lines should be concrete**. Start with a moment, a decision, a specific technical detail. Not a thesis statement.
- **2-3 relevant hashtags max at the end**. #buildinpublic, #indiehackers, #solofounder are fine. Don't use #AI or #innovation.
- **Occasional line breaks for rhythm**. LinkedIn renders them as paragraph breaks. Use for emphasis.
- **End with a question only sometimes**. Overuse signals engagement-bait. Once every 3-4 posts is fine.

## Format that works

### The technical decision reflection (150-250 words)
Opens with the decision. Middle: the tradeoff and what you actually considered. End: what you'd tell yourself 3 months ago.

### The "I was wrong" post (200-300 words)
Opens with the wrong belief. Middle: what forced the update. End: what the corrected belief looks like now.

### The milestone without the hype (100-200 words)
Opens with a number or moment. Middle: what it means, what it doesn't mean. End: what's next. Never celebrate without honesty about scale.

### The prompt engineering deep-cut (250-400 words — longer is fine here)
Opens with the specific problem. Middle: what you tried, what failed, what worked. End: the insight. Builder audience loves this.

## Example — good LinkedIn post

> I almost put a signup wall in front of ArcForge's arc generation.
> 
> Every growth playbook I read said to capture the email first. Every analytics guide said you can't retarget anonymous users. Every instinct I had from building B2B tools screamed "gate it."
> 
> I removed the signup wall two weeks before launch.
> 
> Here's what tipped it: ArcForge's entire growth model is the Arc Card. Users generate a card, screenshot it, post it to TikTok or Discord. If I put a signup between "user arrives" and "user has a shareable card," I'm putting friction in the exact place where the viral loop needs to fire.
> 
> A signup wall assumes your product's value is locked behind the login. ArcForge's value is the artifact that the user wants to show their friends. The artifact has to come first.
> 
> 17 users have generated arcs so far. I don't have their emails. I can't retarget them. Phase 2 adds optional auth for users who want to continue their story into weekly episodes — that's where the signup earns its place, because by then the user has something they want to come back to.
> 
> The wall belongs at the point of ongoing value, not the point of first contact.
> 
> #buildinpublic #indiehackers

## Example — BAD LinkedIn post (avoid)

> 🚀 Excited to share a major milestone! ArcForge just crossed 17 users 🎉
> 
> Here are 3 lessons I learned:
> 
> 1️⃣ Product-led growth wins
> 2️⃣ Gen Z wants authentic experiences
> 3️⃣ AI unlocks personalization at scale
> 
> What are YOUR thoughts on AI-powered personalization? 👇
> 
> #AI #Innovation #Startup #Entrepreneur #GenZ #Anime #BuildInPublic #TechForGood

Why it's bad: emoji spam, listicle, corporate-speak ("product-led growth wins"), vague platitudes ("unlocks personalization at scale"), hashtag spam, engagement-bait question.

## Technical depth calibration

LinkedIn posts CAN include real technical specifics from the repo — schema decisions, rate limiting logic, prompt engineering details, architecture choices. This is a feature, not a bug. Builder LinkedIn audiences want depth.

The line: share decisions and tradeoffs, not secrets. "I chose IP + fingerprint hybrid rate limiting" is fine. Full code dumps are not — save those for Twitter thread format if you want.

When in doubt: would you be comfortable if a competitor read this? If yes, ship it.
